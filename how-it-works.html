How it works

Small pieces of work, properly finished.

Work like this is often sold by the hour, with no clear end. Provenance is set up the other way round. You pick one defined piece of work, and you know what you will get, when, and roughly what it will cost before you say yes.

Once the firm opens

What happens, in order.

A first conversation

About thirty minutes, at no charge. You describe the situation. Provenance says plainly whether it can help and, if it cannot, who might.

A written scope

One page that sets out what will be delivered, by when, and for what fee. Nothing begins until you have agreed it.

Your lawyers, only where needed

Most of this work needs no lawyer at all. Where a question is a legal one, you name who decides it: your own counsel, or a locally bar-registered lawyer Provenance introduces at no cost, for you to assess independently.

The work

Provenance gathers the facts from your documents, your systems and your people, and keeps its requests short. What you share is used for your work and nothing else.

The handover

You receive the finished pack. Any point that needs a legal decision comes on a single list, with the facts behind it, ready for your lawyers.

Payment

Half of the fee is planned to fall due at the start and half on delivery. If anything agreed is missing, it is put right before the second half is due.

Where Provenance stops

Where the work hands over to your lawyers.

Provenance is not a law firm, and it does not give legal advice or draft contracts. It does not need to: almost all of this is compliance and governance work — exactly what the CIPP/E, AIGP and an LL.M. in Data Economy Law are for.

The small part that is reserved to lawyers — drafting or negotiating a contract, going to court — stays with yours. Provenance hands them the facts, so they spend their time on judgment, not fact-finding.

Three commitments

What you can hold Provenance to.

No answer without a source document

Nothing is called a fact without the document, record or number to show it. If a thing cannot be shown, the pack says so.

Never working for both sides of a deal

Sellers and buyers are both welcome, but never on the same deal. If Provenance already works for the other party, it will say so and step back.

Legal decisions stay with your lawyers

No legal advice, no contract wording. Those stay with your lawyers, who get the facts already in order.
