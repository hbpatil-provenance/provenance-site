How it works

You choose one piece of work and pay one fixed fee, agreed in writing before work starts.

Work like this is often billed by the hour, with no clear end and a final spend nobody can predict. Provenance works differently. The fee is fixed for the agreed work and does not grow with the hours it takes. Before you say yes, you know what you get, when you get it and what you pay.

Once the firm opens

What happens, in order.

A first conversation

The first call is free. You describe the problem you are facing, and Provenance tells you how it can help. If Provenance cannot help, it tells you directly and suggests who might, before you have spent anything.

A written scope

Before work starts, you get one page that says what you will receive, when you will receive it and the fixed fee. You decide whether to go ahead, and nothing starts until you agree.

Your lawyers, only where needed

Provenance gathers the facts and prepares the documents. When a question needs a legal decision, the question goes to a lawyer admitted to practise in the country whose law applies: your own lawyer, or one that Provenance introduces to you free of charge. You decide independently whether to hire that lawyer.

The work

Provenance gathers the facts from your documents, your systems and your people, and keeps its requests short. Provenance uses what you share for your work only.

The handover

You receive the finished documents. Every point that needs a legal decision is on one list, with the facts behind it, so your lawyers can spend their time making those decisions rather than collecting facts and evidence.

Payment

For each piece of work, you pay half of the fee at the start and the other half after delivery. If anything agreed is missing, Provenance fixes it before you pay the second half.

Where Provenance stops

Where the work hands over to your lawyers.

Provenance does the compliance and governance work: it finds the facts, prepares the documents and sets up the procedures that EU laws such as the GDPR, the EU AI Act, DORA and the Data Act make necessary. The founder, Hemant Patil, does this work himself. He is a qualified lawyer, although not admitted to any Bar in the EU, and he holds the CIPP/E (data protection) and AIGP (AI governance) certifications. Provenance is not a law firm. Work that national law reserves to lawyers, such as legal advice, drafting and negotiating contracts, and representing a client before a court, stays with lawyers admitted to practise in the country whose law applies.

Before your lawyers start, Provenance gives them the facts and the documents that support each fact.

Three commitments

What you can hold Provenance to.

No answer without a source document

Each answer Provenance prepares points to the contract, policy or record that supports it. For example, an answer about where your customer data is stored points to the hosting contract. If nothing supports an answer yet, Provenance tells you, so you can close the gap before a customer asks.

No conflicts of interest between clients

Provenance works for sellers and for buyers, but never for two clients whose interests conflict, for example the two sides of one deal. If Provenance already works for a company whose interests conflict with yours, Provenance tells you before you share anything and declines the work. What you share is used only for your work and is not shared with another client.

Legal decisions stay with a lawyer admitted to practise where the law applies

Provenance gives no legal advice and writes no contract clauses. Your in-house counsel or your existing external counsel decides each legal question, using the facts and documents Provenance has collected for your counsel. If a question falls under the law of a country where neither is admitted to practise, Provenance can introduce a lawyer who is. The introduction is free, and you are under no obligation to hire that lawyer.
