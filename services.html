For companies that use AI

Use AI across your business, with the documents the AI Act asks for.

Your teams are already using AI, in hiring, in customer service, in decisions about people. The EU AI Act places duties on the company that uses a system as well as the one that built it. The heaviest, for high-risk uses, apply from December 2027. Boards, auditors and customers are asking now.

Provenance takes stock of the AI you use, gathers the facts your lawyers need to classify each system, and builds the assessments, policies and records the Act expects. You are left with something your own people can keep current.

For teams building a product

Month by month

Ongoing privacy and AI support, without a full-time hire.

An assessment is accurate on the day it is finished. Then the product changes, a new tool arrives, a rule is amended. Most growing companies need someone experienced watching this, but not forty hours a week.

For companies based outside the EU

Your EU representative: a named person, not a new office.

If your company has no establishment in the EU but offers goods or services to people there, or monitors their behaviour, Article 27 of the GDPR generally requires a named representative inside the Union. Customers and tenders ask for that name early.

You do not need a subsidiary or an employee to meet it. The representative is appointed in writing, under a service contract. Once Provenance is established in Portugal, it can be that representative: the contact for individuals and authorities, with the record of processing kept alongside. You get a senior person who also understands your systems.

Check that it applies to you. Not every company needs one. The duty falls away where processing is occasional, involves no large-scale sensitive data and is unlikely to put people at risk. It does not apply to public bodies. The representative also has to be in an EU country where some of the people concerned are. Provenance checks both points with you first.

A representative is not a data protection officer and not your legal adviser, and your own responsibility under the GDPR stays with you. Provenance never holds both roles for one client. A similar role exists under the EU AI Act for some AI providers outside the Union. Ask if that may be you.
