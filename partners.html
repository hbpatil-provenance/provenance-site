Four ways to work with Provenance

Provenance does the compliance work. Your lawyers make every legal decision.

This division of roles stays the same whether Provenance joins your product launch, works with your law firm, does one defined job, or answers the questions that return in your EU deals.

For teams building something new

Get a compliance specialist working with your team from the start of your launch.

You are bringing an AI or data product to market, and the open questions are multiplying: what the EU AI Act requires, where the data may be sent, what your first customers will ask to see. No one in your company is responsible for these questions yet.

Provenance joins the project and handles compliance and governance, from the first design decisions to the documents your first customers ask for. When you are ready, Provenance hands the work over, and your team runs it from then on.

What that looks like

  • A compliance lead working with your team on the launch
  • The assessments, policies and records your product needs
  • A handover, so your own team can run the work after launch

For law firms

Provenance does the compliance work for your clients. You stay their lawyer.

Your clients want the operational side of GDPR, EU AI Act or DORA work: registers, assessments, evidence documents and answers to customer questionnaires. This work is hard to staff inside a law firm, so the client takes it elsewhere.

Provenance does this operational work for your firm, under your direction. The client stays your client, every legal decision stays with you, and the client gets the whole compliance job done. The roles stay separate: Provenance establishes the facts and prepares the documents and procedures, and your firm gives the legal advice.

What that looks like

  • The practical compliance work, done under your direction
  • A clear boundary: Provenance never gives legal advice
  • One senior point of contact, on demand
  • Take on the whole client project without hiring more people

For companies that need a result, not a hire

Get one specific job done by a senior specialist, without hiring.

Sometimes you need one specific thing done well by someone senior, without hiring another employee.

Engage Provenance for one defined result: a file of facts for a data transfer assessment, a completed EU AI Act assessment, or an appointed EU representative. Provenance sets the scope and the fixed fee before work starts, and the work ends when the result is delivered.

Where a matter needs advice on the law of one country, you can use your own lawyer, or Provenance introduces a lawyer admitted to practise in that country, free of charge, for you to assess independently.

What that looks like

  • A named senior specialist for one result
  • A fixed scope and fee, agreed first
  • Your own lawyer, or a free introduction to a lawyer in the country concerned, where national law is needed

For law firms and technology companies

Handle EU deals without redoing the same work each time.

You have a steady number of EU deals, and the same data and AI questions come up in each one. Each time, someone on your team writes the answers again.

Provenance keeps one set of evidence documents for your product and updates the set when the product changes. When a new customer sends questions, the answers are already written and linked to documents. Your sales team replies with confidence and gives the same answer every time.

What that looks like

  • One set of evidence documents for your product, kept up to date
  • Less repeated work on each new deal
  • A partner who knows your product and your customers’ questions

How it stays simple

What Provenance does, and what your lawyers do.

In each of these four arrangements, Provenance does the compliance and governance work: gathering the facts, setting up the procedures and preparing the evidence. Legal decisions stay with lawyers: your in-house team, your law firm, or a lawyer admitted to practise in the relevant country whom Provenance introduces free of charge, if you wish.

This clear division of roles makes a partnership easy to agree to and easy to explain to your own clients.
