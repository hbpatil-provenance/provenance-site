Which of these do you need now?

AI Governance

For companies that use, build or buy AI.

Use AI in your business, with the documents the EU AI Act requires.

Hiring, credit, customer service, internal tools: if you use AI in these, the EU AI Act can place duties on you, not only on the company that built the AI. Provenance gathers the facts your lawyers need to decide which duties apply and prepares the documents that show how you handle them.

Governing the AI you use

Launch your AI product without having to rebuild it later for EU legal provisions.

The EU AI Act, the GDPR (EU data protection law) and your customers’ contract terms affect one another. Provenance reviews all three together, early in the project, and turns the results into product decisions.

How a delayed deal moves forward

Sell to banks and insurers: comply with the DORA provisions they require from their suppliers.

DORA is the EU law on digital operational resilience for banks, insurers and other financial companies. Banks and insurers must pass its requirements for suppliers on in their contracts, so those requirements are now in your contract. Provenance compares each requirement with what you can show today and marks each gap it finds. Estimating the cost of closing a gap, and closing it, are separate work.

For the GDPR, the EU AI Act, DORA, the Data Act and NIS2 (the EU network security law), Provenance delivers documents that are easy to read: a question, an answer, and the document, record or number behind the answer.

Who leads Provenance

Hemant Patil has spent twenty-four years answering the questions buyers ask before they sign.

Provenance is led by Hemant Patil. He holds the CIPP/E (data protection) and AIGP (AI governance), two of the leading professional certifications in their fields, and is completing an LL.M. (master of laws) in Data Economy Law.

Before founding Provenance, Hemant Patil spent twenty-four years working in enterprise technology, twelve of them at Oracle and IBM. He worked in more than forty countries. Customers often asked him for proof that what they were buying met their requirements before they would sign. Today the GDPR, which already applies, and the EU AI Act, whose duties are phasing in, require the companies they cover to be able to show how their data and AI practices meet the legal provisions of those laws, and customers ask for the same proof. The GDPR and the EU AI Act can also apply to companies based outside the EU, when they offer goods or services to people in the EU or their AI is used there. Customers, boards and authorities ask such companies to demonstrate how they comply with these laws. He prepares the documents that show how a company handles data and AI.
